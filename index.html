PandaWare v2

StatusUpdatedSheet
In CAD, first finger printingOct 20262 of 5

v1 couples its finger joints, so each finger curls as one unit. That's enough to grab an object and not enough to reach a defined hand pose. v2 drives the MCP, PIP, and DIP joints of every finger separately, with passive spring return, so mechanical coupling stops being a source of positioning error.

  1. Independent joint actuation

    Each joint gets its own flexor tendon with spring return, so a commanded pose is a set of joint angles instead of one shared curl. Dropping antagonist extensors halves the tendon count and the friction that comes with it.

  2. Ball-bearing joints

    Printed pin joints wear and loosen, so their friction changes from cycle to cycle. A bearing at each joint keeps friction low and the same every time, which is what makes motion repeatable.

    • 1 Ball bearing
    • 2 Joint shaft, on the joint axis
    • 3 Tendon channel

  3. Rigid skeleton, soft contact

    The skeleton is printed in nylon or PETG to hold its geometry under tendon load, with TPU 85A on the gripping faces. Stiffness goes where precision matters and give goes where the hand touches things.

  4. Angle sensing on every joint

    An AS5600 magnetic encoder reads a diametric magnet on the end of each joint shaft. The hand measures the pose it actually reached instead of trusting the servo command, which turns positioning error into a number.

Where it stands

  1. Finger module Independent MCP, PIP, and DIP joints in Onshape.
  2. Tendon routing Paths for three flexor tendons per finger.
  3. Bearing seats Built into the joint geometry.
  4. First print Printing and assembling the first finger.
  5. Precision testing Joint-by-joint error against defined hand poses.

EMG assistive glove

StatusTargetSheet
Building the first finger jointISEF 20273 of 5

A glove that reads forearm muscle signals and helps people with weak grip close their hand. Most assistive gloves leave the thumb passive, which rules out most everyday grasps.

What's different

Powered thumb
The thumb is driven in two directions, so it can oppose the fingers instead of being dragged along.
Force-aware grip
Motor current tells the glove when it has made contact, so it stops before crushing something delicate.
Flat objects
Flexor and extensor cables pull against each other to hold flat things that usually slip out of tendon gloves.

How it moves

Small geared motors sit on the forearm and pull cables through Bowden sheaths, so the hand carries only a light skeleton. Hinges on the side of the finger line up with the real knuckle axis, and a magnet in each hinge pin lets the glove measure its own joint angles.

An EMG-controlled prosthetic hand I built from scratch. It reads forearm muscle signals, decides in real time on an RP2040 whether you're trying to grip, and drives five tendon-routed fingers with MG996R servos. A current sensor stops the grip when a finger hits an object.

Known limitations

The current-based grip stop works on rigid objects but not soft ones, where the current rises too slowly to catch cleanly. The finger joints are coupled, so the hand can grasp but can't form a specific pose. EMG drifts with electrode placement, sweat, and fatigue; calibration reduces that but doesn't remove it.

Parts and why

AssemblySheet
PandaWare v15 of 5

Every part was picked for a reason. Open a row to see it.

ItemPartRole
1RP2040Microcontroller

Picked over an Arduino Uno because EMG sampling and servo control have to run without blocking each other, and the RP2040's two cores made that possible for $4. The tradeoff is no floating-point hardware, which limits how much signal processing can happen on the chip.

2MicroPythonFirmware

Picked for iteration speed. Changing a filter constant and rerunning right away mattered more than raw speed, because the bottleneck was the mechanism settling, not compute.

3MyoWare 2.0EMG sensor

Surface EMG was the only intent signal available without implants or a lab budget. Its output drifts with electrode placement, skin, sweat, and fatigue, which is what drove the custom calibration pipeline.

4PCA9685Servo driver

Moves servo timing onto dedicated hardware over I²C, so the microcontroller can sample EMG continuously instead of juggling PWM. Drives every finger servo from two pins.

5INA219Current sensor

The grip-safety system depends on this one. Fingertip force sensors were out of budget, so the hand infers contact from the jump in servo current when a finger stalls against something. It works on rigid objects and struggles on soft ones.

6MG996R servo, ×5Actuators

Metal-gear servos picked for holding position under load rather than speed. One per finger, each pulling a tendon through the printed finger channels.

7PLA+, FDM printedStructure

Printed on a partly broken consumer printer, so I designed around inconsistent tolerances instead of assuming them. Wall thickness and pin reinforcement were sized for print reliability, not just strength.

8Tendon cablesTransmission

Tendons move the actuators off the fingers, which is the only way to keep the fingers close to human size. The cost is friction and slack along the path, which caps positioning precision and is exactly what v2 is built to fix.
